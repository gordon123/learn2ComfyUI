### Slow Motion

**Definition:** Slow motion cinematography plays action over more screen time than the event occupied during capture, usually by overcranking (recording 120 to 240 fps and playing at 24). The extra samples reveal fabric delay, particle hang, and impact mechanics that a 24 fps shutter would compress into a few smeared frames.

**Prompt:** A physically plausible mid-action pose with hanging dust, delayed fabric, and readable contacts, not a statue.

### Fast Motion

**Definition:** Fast motion cinematography compresses screen duration so action plays faster than it occurred, by undercranking the camera or by retiming a normal-rate take. Crowds smear, clouds race, and labor can look absurdly efficient because too many events occupy too little time.

**Prompt:** A pose that implies acceleration (smeared extras, streaked traffic) while the subject, if they must stay readable, holds.

### Speed Ramp

**Definition:** Speed ramping changes playback or capture speed inside a single shot rather than holding one retiming ratio. Approach and recovery stay energetic while one motivated instant (impact, glance, turn) stretches, then the clock returns.

**Prompt:** Real-time approach, a stretched beat, and a recovery — three readable phases implied in one pose.

### Freeze Frame

**Definition:** A freeze frame holds one image so narrative time stops while camera, subject, and world all cease to generate new samples. The hold can end a story, land a joke, or present a face as evidence.

**Prompt:** The chosen frame as a photograph with grain, a decisive expression or body shape, no implied smear.

### Time-Lapse

**Definition:** Time-lapse cinematography captures frames at long intervals so slow change plays as continuous motion, hours becoming seconds as shadows sweep and crowds become flow. It is interval photography, not a sped-up ordinary take.

**Prompt:** Matched architecture at one implied phase, or a composite of sun angles only if you label it a study.

### Step Printing

**Definition:** Step printing repeats or omits frames optically or digitally so motion advances in visible temporal steps. Combined with low-rate capture and long exposures, each frame smears internally while the sequence stutters forward, a texture of delay rather than clean high-frame-rate slow motion.

**Prompt:** Duplicated frames and in-frame smear, not 120 fps — a readable figure with strongly smeared passersby, long-exposure street texture.

### Reverse Motion

**Definition:** Reverse motion plays recorded action backward so causes arrive after their effects inside the shot: rain rises, smoke inhales, a shattered object reassembles. This is reversed playback, not a story told in reverse scene order.

**Prompt:** Inverted physics, not a shuffled outline — a tell such as rain hanging upward, glass mid-reassembly, smoke drawing in.

### Bullet Time

**Definition:** Bullet time freezes or radically slows action while the viewpoint travels around it, decoupling camera speed from subject time. The instant becomes inspectable space: a time-slice orbit around a sculpture of speed, not ordinary slow motion with a handheld camera.

**Prompt:** Two clocks: action stopped or crushed, camera continuing on a path — one instant seen as if from a step along an orbit, hanging debris, consistent anatomy.

### Long Take

**Definition:** Long take cinematography is an unusually extended shot that preserves continuous performance, space, action, or duration without an overt cut. Blocking and camera share one sentence so the audience cannot leave the room.

**Prompt:** Unbroken duration and what happens in depth — a wide ensemble with a readable route, not proof of a take.

### Oner

**Definition:** A oner shot is designed to play as if the scene were one take, with no visible edits. It may be a true single take or a stitched oner whose joins hide in whips, darkness, or crossing bodies.

**Prompt:** A whole scene as one take — a frame from the path, not proof.

### Motion Blur

**Definition:** Motion blur cinematography is the streaking or softness caused by subject or camera movement during exposure. The longer the gate stays open relative to the motion, the more the image paints, so edges fail on purpose.

**Prompt:** Commit to streaks — lamps as ribbons, bodies smearing, a subject sharp only if they held still.

### Stutter / Stop-Stutter

**Definition:** Stutter editing drops or repeats frames so playback skips and the world's clock hitches. It is an anxious rhythm, not the lyric smear of step printing and not a jump cut's spatial jump.

**Prompt:** Frames dropping or repeating, not smear and not a jump in angle — a face or gesture that looks temporally sampled, slight after-images only if they come from repeats.

### Boomerang

**Definition:** A boomerang video effect plays a short action forward, then snaps it backward along the same samples, often looping. The gesture comes and goes on one path: reversed playback used as a round trip rather than a one-way undo.

**Prompt:** Write the round trip — the mid-gesture peak, because a still cannot boomerang.

### Infinite Loop

**Definition:** An infinite loop video sews the last frame to the first so motion never arrives at an ending. The cycle can be a seamless visual join, or, in narrative use, a repeated day whose variables change.

**Prompt:** A seamless visual sew or a repeated-day story — a pose that could be both start and end.

### Stop Motion

**Definition:** Stop motion cinematography photographs physical objects incrementally, adjusting them between exposures so successive poses create apparent movement. The cadence is pose to pose and slightly staccato, with tactile materials in the light.

**Prompt:** Materials and increments, not a filter — a puppet or object mid-pose, visible construction, coherent joints.

### Low Shutter

**Definition:** Low shutter cinematography uses a short shutter angle or short exposure so moving edges stay sharp inside each frame. Normal-rate action then feels abrupt and staccato because this is reduced motion blur, not a long-exposure smear.

**Prompt:** Short shutter, not long smear — crisp droplets and defined moving limbs in a tense handheld frame.

### Timelapse Glam

**Definition:** Timelapse glam is interval photography of a styling process in which clothes, hair, and makeup change at high speed while the face holds. The person is a still axis and wardrobe is the changing weather.

**Prompt:** Lock the face, accelerate the styling — one held identity with evidence of process (hands, garments in mid-swap).

### Timelapse Human

**Definition:** Timelapse human holds one person in living time while the crowd is interval-sampled into smoke, so one still will stands in a rushing system. It is the opposite clock of a frozen world with a moving hero.

**Prompt:** Hold the person, lapse the crowd — one sharp figure, pedestrians as flow, stable sun unless you want a day.

### Timelapse Landscape

**Definition:** Timelapse landscape interval-samples light and weather over a place so hours play in seconds as clouds and shadows sweep. Geography is the actor, and a person, if present, is a scale mark rather than a racing extra.

**Prompt:** Light and weather as the motion — one honest sun angle on stable geology.

### Frozen in Motion

**Definition:** Frozen in motion stops the environment and most people while a chosen character, the viewpoint, or both continue to move. Particles, extras, and pigeons hang, and the rule of who may move is the entire effect.

**Prompt:** Name who moves — one active subject in a square of frozen pedestrians and hanging debris.

### Moonwalk

**Definition:** A moonwalk effect is a backward glide that reads as a forward myth: the torso stays upright, one foot slides, the other pushes, and the heel does not lift like a walk. It is a locomotion lie in living time, not reversed footage.

**Prompt:** Write the slide, not a reverse clip — mid-glide, one foot flat, torso upright, coat trailing.
