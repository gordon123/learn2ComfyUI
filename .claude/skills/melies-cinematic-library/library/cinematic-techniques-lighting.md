### Three-Point Lighting

**Definition:** Three-point lighting, the basic three point lighting setup, places a key, a fill, and a backlight so a face is modeled from one dominant side, opened in the shadows, and separated from the background. The key is brightest and sets direction; fill and back exist only as ratios against it.

**Prompt:** Name all three jobs, not the word portrait — add key side (left or right), fill ratio, and backlight color.

### Key Light

**Definition:** A key light in key light cinematography is the principal source that sets the subject's dominant direction, shape, and exposure. Shadows fall away from it; every other light is measured against it.

**Prompt:** Name the source and the side before any style word — add height (high, eye, table), hardness, and Kelvin.

### Fill Light

**Definition:** Fill light reduces contrast or adds detail in areas the key does not reach, usually from the opposite side and at a lower level. It is a ratio tool, not a second sun.

**Prompt:** Say which cheek opens and how little — add 2:1 or 4:1 if you need a number.

### Backlight

**Definition:** Backlight in backlight cinematography is light coming from behind the subject toward the camera, tracing edges and lifting the figure off the background. It may stay a thin rim or, in haze, become a visible shaft.

**Prompt:** Put the source behind the body, not on the nose — name hair, coat edge, and whether haze is present.

### Rim Light

**Definition:** Rim lighting is a narrow edge of light, usually from a flagged backlight, that separates a subject from the background without opening the face. The highlight sits on brim, hair, and coat; flesh stays mostly dark.

**Prompt:** Photographic backlight on [Subject] from behind, a thin natural highlight on hat brim, hair, and coat edge, faces staying dark.

### Kicker

**Definition:** A kicker light is a relatively hard side-back accent on cheek, jaw, shoulder, or an object edge, placed behind and to one side of the subject. It is a slice, not a full wrap and not a hair-only rim.

**Prompt:** A small warm lamp behind and to the side of [Subject], a thin highlight on the far jaw and coat shoulder only.

### Hair Light

**Definition:** A hair light is a small source behind and above the subject that lights only the top of the hair as a rim so texture separates from the background. The face stays on the key or in cooler shadow.

**Prompt:** A small lamp behind and above [Subject], lighting only the top of the hair as a rim.

### Eye Light

**Definition:** An eye light in eye light cinematography is a small frontal source used to put a catchlight in the irises or to lift the eyes a fraction without becoming a fill. The face can stay in shadow; the eyes remain human, not lamps.

**Prompt:** A dark portrait of [Subject], face mostly in shadow, a tiny white reflection of a lamp sitting in each human iris.

### High-Key Lighting

**Definition:** High-key lighting, or high key lighting, uses relatively low contrast, bright exposure, and limited deep shadow so the frame stays open and readable. It is a contrast ratio, not a command to overexpose.

**Prompt:** Ask for low contrast and protected whites, not a flash — add large sources and say highlights hold texture.

### Low-Key Lighting

**Definition:** Low-key lighting, or low key lighting, uses high contrast, selective illumination, and substantial shadow so most of the frame can sit near black. One pool of light is often enough; the dark is designed, not an accident of underexposure.

**Prompt:** Name the pool and the black — name the single source and forbid extra fill.

### Chiaroscuro

**Definition:** Chiaroscuro lighting uses strong light-dark contrast to model volume and to create moral, psychological, or dramatic tension. Darkness withholds; the lit plane is what the scene will admit.

**Prompt:** One source, fast falloff, modeled planes — name the lamp.

### Rembrandt Lighting

**Definition:** Rembrandt lighting, in rembrandt lighting terms, is a portrait pattern in which the key sits high and about 45 degrees off axis so the nose shadow joins the shadow cheek, leaving a small triangle of light under the shadow-side eye. The rest of that cheek stays dark.

**Prompt:** Demand the triangle by name — say the nose shadow connects to the cheek shadow.

### Butterfly Lighting

**Definition:** Butterfly lighting, also called Paramount lighting, places the key high and on the camera axis so a small butterfly-shaped shadow falls under the nose on the philtrum. Both cheeks stay open; both eyes take light.

**Prompt:** Keep the key on center and high — forbid a shadow cheek.

### Loop Lighting

**Definition:** Loop lighting creates a small nose shadow that loops toward the corner of the mouth and does not connect to the cheek shadow. The key sits slightly off axis and a little high, a middle step between butterfly and Rembrandt.

**Prompt:** Loop lighting on [Subject], key slightly off-axis, a small loop shadow from the nose toward the corner of the mouth — add that the loop does not touch the cheek shadow.

### Split Lighting

**Definition:** Split lighting divides the face into a lit half and a shadow half by placing the key at about 90 degrees to the nose, a vertical cut down the center line. There is no Rembrandt triangle and no loop; the far plane is largely gone.

**Prompt:** Split lighting on [Subject], one half of the face in light, the other in shadow, a vertical moral cut — add key at 90 degrees, almost no fill, no triangle on the dark cheek.

### Short Lighting

**Definition:** Short lighting in short lighting terms keys the side of the face turned away from camera, so the camera sees more shadow than light. The head looks slimmer and more dimensional because the near cheek falls off.

**Prompt:** Short lighting on [Subject], the side turned away from camera is keyed, the near side falls toward shadow, slimming and dramatic — add that the camera-facing cheek is the darker one.

### Broad Lighting

**Definition:** Broad lighting keys the side of the face turned toward camera, so the near cheek is the bright one and the shadow sits on the far side. The face reads open, public, and less mysterious.

**Prompt:** Broad lighting on [Subject], the camera-facing side keyed, open and public, less shadow on the near cheek — say the bright plane is the one occupying most of the frame.

### Silhouette

**Definition:** A silhouette shot places the subject dark against a brighter background so the figure reads as shape, not as a modeled face. Gesture and contour do the work; surface detail is gone.

**Prompt:** Expose the ground, starve the body — name a readable outline (limbs separated).

### Motivated Lighting

**Definition:** Motivated lighting is designed to appear consistent with a visible or plausible story-world source: a window, a lamp, a sign, the sky. Hidden units may exist, but they match that source in direction, hardness, and color.

**Prompt:** Justify every beam — name the story source, its side, hardness, and Kelvin.

### Practical Lighting

**Definition:** Practical lighting uses a visible or logically present light source within the set as a real contributor: lamps, neon, candles, screens. You see the fixture, and it (or a unit matching it) actually lights the face.

**Prompt:** Practical lighting on [Subject] from visible lamps, neon, candles, or screens in frame, mixed color temperatures, no hidden movie lights — name which fixture is the key and its color.

### Available Light

**Definition:** Available light in available light cinematography is the illumination already at the location before the crew adds or changes units: sun, sky, or existing interior fixtures. You commit to the hour and the existing contrast.

**Prompt:** Forbid added units — name the hour and the existing source.

### Naturalistic Ambient

**Definition:** Naturalistic ambient lighting wraps the subject in soft, directionally plausible room or sky tone so the craft reads as unstyled even when units are added. Shadows stay believable; nothing announces a movie lamp.

**Prompt:** Ask for room tone, not a named portrait pattern — name a window side.

### Bounce Light

**Definition:** Bounce lighting reflects light from a surface (wall, ceiling, poly, card) before it reaches the subject, so the apparent source becomes that large surface. Direction remains, but shadow edges soften and wrap.

**Prompt:** Name the reflecting surface — state the wall's side and color.

### Hard Light

**Definition:** Hard light in hard light cinematography comes from a small apparent source and produces crisp shadow edges and specular definition on skin, metal, and walls. Texture is amplified; falloff can still be steep or long depending on distance.

**Prompt:** Hard light on [Subject] from a small distant source, razor shadow edges, texture amplified, noir graphic — forbid silks, wrap, and cloudy softness unless weather is the point.

### Soft Light

**Definition:** Soft light in soft light cinematography comes from a large apparent source and produces gradual shadow transitions and wrap around the face or body. Speculars get larger and dimmer; texture is reduced.

**Prompt:** Name a large source and gentle edges — keep a key side.

### Side Lighting

**Definition:** Side lighting keys primarily from one side, around 90 degrees to the camera-subject line, so shape and texture are emphasized. Half the form goes toward light, half toward shadow.

**Prompt:** Put the lamp at 90 degrees — name hardness.

### Top Light

**Definition:** Top lighting is illumination from directly above the subject, falling on forehead, nose bridge, and shoulders while the eye sockets and the underside of the chin go into shadow. The source is a ceiling, a rig, or a high practical, not a frontal beauty key.

**Prompt:** A lamp directly above [Subject], light falling on forehead and shoulders, eye sockets in shadow, human eyes, no light emitting from the pupils — forbid frontal beauty fill.

### Underlighting

**Definition:** Underlighting, also called uplighting, lights from below the face so shadows climb the cheekbones and the tops of the eye sockets. The modeling is the reverse of daylight and often reads as uncanny.

**Prompt:** A lamp on the table below [Subject]'s chin, pointing up.

### Cross Lighting

**Definition:** Cross lighting keys subjects from opposite side directions, so each person or each side of a body gets a key and a far rim. It is common in dialogue coverage, stage, and concert work.

**Prompt:** Name two opposing keys — specify colors if they differ.

### Window Light

**Definition:** Window light in window light cinematography is illumination shaped to appear as light entering through a window: a large, directional source with falloff into the room. The architecture is the modifier.

**Prompt:** Window light as the only key on [Subject], large soft directional source, falloff into the room, painterly interior — name which wall holds the glass and whether the sky is overcast or late sun.

### Candlelight

**Definition:** Candlelight in candlelight cinematography is a small warm practical or a simulated flame with rapid falloff, often around 1800K, that carves faces near the flame and lets the rest of the room die. Flicker is part of the source, not a grade.

**Prompt:** Keep the flame small and close — cluster the candles, kill electric fill, and let corners go dark.

### Neon Practicals

**Definition:** Neon practicals are visible neon or similar sign tubes used as actual keys or strong accents, coloring the subject with magenta, cyan, or other tube hues instead of a hidden white source. The city fixture is the lamp.

**Prompt:** Make the signs the keys — name which tube hits which cheek.

### Golden Hour

**Definition:** Golden hour in golden hour cinematography is low-angle warm sunlight shortly after sunrise or before sunset, when the sun is near the horizon. Shadows go long; the light is directional, warm, and brief.

**Prompt:** Keep the sun low and real — say the sun is just above the horizon.

### Blue Hour

**Definition:** Blue hour in blue hour cinematography is cool ambient twilight before sunrise or after sunset, when the sun is below the horizon but the sky still glows. There is no hard sunlight; the sky is a large cool source, and warm practicals often kick in.

**Prompt:** Blue hour wrap on [Subject], deep cobalt sky still glowing, warm practicals kicking in — forbid a visible sun disk and a black night sky with no residual light.

### Dappled Light

**Definition:** Dappled light is broken patches of brightness and shadow caused by foliage, water, blinds, or another irregular obstruction in a hard beam. The pattern is organic and often moving.

**Prompt:** Sun through leaves on [Subject], irregular patches of brightness and leaf-shaped shadows on coat and horse, a real tree overhead — forbid a clean unpatterned key and a venetian-bar prison graphic unless blinds are the story.

### Gobo Lighting

**Definition:** Gobo lighting places a pattern or object in a light beam (a gobo or cucoloris) so the source throws graphic shadows across face, body, and wall. The room writes on the person.

**Prompt:** Name the object in the beam — specify a hard source.

### Volumetric Light

**Definition:** Volumetric lighting makes beams visible through haze, fog, dust, smoke, or other particles, so the air photographs as well as the subject. Also called god rays or crepuscular rays when the shafts are distinct.

**Prompt:** Name source plus medium — attach every beam to a window, sun, or searchlight.

### Cameo Lighting

**Definition:** Cameo lighting isolates a lit subject against a predominantly dark environment, so the figure sits in a pool and the world falls to black. The person remains modeled; the set is withheld.

**Prompt:** Kill the room — keep the face modeled.

### Epiphany

**Definition:** Epiphany lighting is a change of illumination that makes a face or figure suddenly the brightest readable fact in the room as a realization lands. The light finds the person; it is timed to understanding, not left as a static setup.

**Prompt:** Still: "Epiphany light on [Subject], a sudden intelligible brightness, as if the thought itself switched a lamp." Video: "Light finds [Subject] as a realization lands, the face becoming the brightest fact in the room." Name what caused the change (sun, door, lamp).

### Spotlight

**Definition:** A spotlight in spotlight cinematography is a focused, hard-edged pool from a theatrical or similar instrument, with darkness around the circle. The person is treated as an act inside that pool.

**Prompt:** Demand a hard pool and a dark surround — mention a visible circle or shuttered edge.

### Glam

**Definition:** Glam lighting, in glam lighting terms, is a large beauty key with catchlights in the eyes and a little diffusion on skin, built to sell the face. It is a package of size, frontal or near-frontal placement, and skin treatment, not a single nose-shadow pattern.

**Prompt:** Soft beauty key on [Subject], large source, catchlights inside the eyes, a little diffusion on skin.
