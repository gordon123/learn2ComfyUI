### Teal and Orange

**Definition:** A teal and orange color grade pushes shadows and often skies toward cyan-teal while keeping skin and warm sources in the orange half of the wheel. It is a complementary split with faces protected, not a silver-retention process and not a general cool wash.

**Prompt:** Teal-and-orange grade on [Subject], skin staying warm, shadows and skies pushed cyan, complementary split.

### Bleach Bypass

**Definition:** Bleach bypass retains silver during processing, or simulates that retention, so the dye image sits on leftover metallic density. Contrast rises, grain shows, saturation falls, and highlights turn harsh rather than candy-colored.

**Prompt:** Bleach-bypass look on [Subject], retained silver, crushed color, harsh contrast, metallic midtones.

### Monochrome

**Definition:** Monochrome, including black and white cinematography, removes or does not record chromatic information so the image is value, texture, and shape. Hue is gone; contrast, grain, and outline do the talking.

**Prompt:** Monochrome treatment of [Subject], silver highlights, deep blacks, no chroma, shape and light only.

### Sepia

**Definition:** Sepia tone in sepia tone film maps the grey scale onto brown-gold instead of neutral silver, so whites go toward faded paper and blacks toward umber. It is a color of the past as chemistry, not a warm grade on a full-hue world.

**Prompt:** Sepia tonality on [Subject], brown-gold memory, faded whites, the past as a color, not a filter sticker.

### Desaturation

**Definition:** Desaturation in desaturated cinematography reduces color intensity while leaving hue present. The world is wintered or dusted; leftover accents still speak, but chroma is no longer loud.

**Prompt:** Desaturated palette on [Subject], chroma pulled back, dusty, wintered, emotion in leftover color accents.

### Hyper-Saturation

**Definition:** Hyper-saturation in saturated cinematography raises color intensity past ordinary record, so hues read as lacquer, fever, or dream. Feeling is louder than fact; the palette is not documentary.

**Prompt:** Hyper-saturated color on [Subject], Velvia intensity, reds like lacquer, a dream or a fever, not documentary.

### Cool Blue

**Definition:** A cool blue grade in cool color grade work shifts the image toward steel, cyan, or moonlight blues across shadows and often midtones. Warmth, if it survives, is reserved for a practical or a face that the story wants to save.

**Prompt:** Cool blue grade on [Subject], steel shadows, hospital or moonlight, warmth reserved for one practical if any.

### Warm Amber

**Definition:** A warm amber grade in warm color grade work biases mids and highlights toward tungsten honey, candle, and practical glow. Safety, home, or nostalgia sits in the mids; the world is invited rather than chilled.

**Prompt:** Warm amber grade on [Subject], tungsten honey, candle and practical glow, safety or nostalgia in the mids.

### Filmic Faded

**Definition:** A filmic faded grade in filmic color grade work imitates a print: blacks are lifted off true zero, highlights roll off instead of clipping like video, and contrast sits like photochemical display. It is not HDR punch, and it is not automatically period costume.

**Prompt:** Filmic faded grade on [Subject], lifted blacks, rolled-off highlights, print-emulation, slightly expired romance.

### Cross Process

**Definition:** A cross process look imitates developing a film in the wrong chemistry, such as slide stock in color-negative developer, so primaries skew off their true identities. Contrast often climbs; cyan shadows, magenta or yellow skin shifts, and other chemical accidents become the palette.

**Prompt:** Cross-processed look on [Subject], skewed primaries, cyan shadows, magenta skin shift, music-video chemical accident.

### Day for Night

**Definition:** Day for night cinematography photographs daylight so it will read as nighttime while still showing terrain that true night might hide. Exposure, sky, and highlight control do the lying; a blue wash alone is not the technique.

**Prompt:** Day-for-night treatment of [Subject], underexposed daylight, blue wash, skipped-over sun, moonlight as a lie that reads.

### Tungsten Balance

**Definition:** Tungsten balance in tungsten color temperature work sets the camera or stock for about 3200K so tungsten practicals render near-neutral or gently warm while daylight and cooler sources go blue. Different source colors remain visibly distinct rather than being normalized to one white.

**Prompt:** Tungsten-balanced interior on [Subject], 3200K warmth, windows going cold blue, mixed sources telling inside versus out.

### Moonlight Gel

**Definition:** Moonlight gel in moonlight lighting gel work is a cool, often cyan or steel-green key standing in for the moon, usually a large source from above or through trees. Night stays sculpted: there is a direction, a rim, and deep underexposure, not only a blue grade.

**Prompt:** Moonlight gel on [Subject], cyan-green night, a single cool key as the moon, deep underexposure, silver edges.

### Split Toning

**Definition:** Split toning in split toning cinematography assigns one hue to highlights and another to shadows, so the tone curve becomes a two-color world. Any pair is legal; teal-and-orange is only one famous instance.

**Prompt:** Split-toned grade on [Subject], highlights one hue, shadows another, for example gold over teal, a two-color world.

### Natural Grade

**Definition:** A natural color grade keeps skin faithful and saturation restrained so the location's real color science remains the authority. No stylized split, no silver punishment, no fever: the grade mostly refuses to editorialize hue.

**Prompt:** Natural grade on [Subject], faithful skin, restrained saturation, no stylized split, the location's real color science.

### Color Shift

**Definition:** A color shift effect is a change of color state that the audience is meant to notice: monochrome to hue, one world to another, or a slide of temperature during the shot. Color is the event, not a hidden LUT.

**Prompt:** Color shift across [Subject], hues sliding mid-shot or mid-grade, a world that changes temperature in front of us.

### Vintage

**Definition:** A vintage film look treats the image as past chemistry: faded dyes, possible halation, softened contrast, and period color that has drifted. It is a whole-file costume of time, not only old glass and not only a lifted print curve.

**Prompt:** Vintage treatment of [Subject], faded dyes, halation, the past as chemistry, not a sticker.

### Palette

**Definition:** A color palette in color palette cinematography is a restricted set of hues used as law across set, costume, light, and grade. Too many colors would be noise; a few chosen hues do all the work and can mark location, timeline, or story state.

**Prompt:** Restricted palette on [Subject], a few chosen hues doing all the work, design as law.

### Overexposed

**Definition:** Overexposed cinematography lets values clip or bloom toward white on purpose so information in the highlights is burned. It is a choice to lose the top of the scale, not the same as high-key lighting, which can stay bright while still holding detail.

**Prompt:** Overexposed [Subject], values clipping toward heaven or fluorescent hell, information burned on purpose.
