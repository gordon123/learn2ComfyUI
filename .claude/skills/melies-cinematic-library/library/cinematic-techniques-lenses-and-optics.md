### 14mm Ultra-Wide

**Definition:** A 14mm ultra-wide in 14mm cinematography is a very short focal length that, from a close stand, takes in a huge field and exaggerates near-to-far size. The close camera position, not the millimeter stamp alone, is what balloons a foreground face or object against a receding room.

**Prompt:** 14mm ultra-wide on [Subject], exaggerated perspective, looming foreground, stretched architecture, slight heroic distortion.

### 24mm Wide

**Definition:** A 24mm wide in 24mm lens cinematography is a moderately wide prime that holds a person and a usable amount of place without extreme stretch. Body and architecture share the rectangle; neither fully wins.

**Prompt:** 24mm wide on [Subject], environmental portrait, body in space, moderate stretch, reportage coverage.

### 35mm Moderate Wide

**Definition:** A 35mm moderate wide in 35mm cinematography is the common narrative wide-normal: natural perspective with a little more world than a 50mm. It is default coverage for scenes that need both face and room.

**Prompt:** 35mm on [Subject], storyteller focal length, natural perspective with a little more world than 50.

### 50mm Normal

**Definition:** A 50mm normal in 50mm cinematography is the conventional normal prime: from a typical stand it neither stretches near space nor stacks distant planes. The glass refuses to editorialize perspective; camera position still does all of that work.

**Prompt:** 50mm normal lens on [Subject], human-scale perspective, neither compressed nor stretched, conversational truth.

### 85mm Portrait

**Definition:** An 85mm portrait in 85mm cinematography is a short telephoto used from a farther stand so a head-and-shoulders stays large while the background enlarges and recedes in importance. The person becomes the landscape; the room becomes stacked color and shape behind them.

**Prompt:** 85mm portrait of [Subject], compressed background, rapid falloff, isolation, the face as the only country.

### 135mm Telephoto

**Definition:** A 135mm telephoto in 135mm cinematography is a long lens used from a distant stand so planes stack and the background crowds the figure. The image feels like eavesdropping: you are far away, yet the world presses in.

**Prompt:** 135mm telephoto on [Subject], stacked planes, eavesdropping compression, background crowding the figure.

### 200mm Long Telephoto

**Definition:** A 200mm long telephoto in 200mm cinematography is an extreme long lens used from a very distant stand so size differences between planes almost vanish. Heat shimmer, stacked terrain, and sniper intimacy are the physical leftovers of that throw.

**Prompt:** 200mm long lens on [Subject], extreme compression, heat shimmer, isolated from a distance, sniper intimacy.

### Anamorphic

**Definition:** Anamorphic cinematography squeezes one image dimension during capture, usually horizontally by 2x, then expands it for presentation. Depending on the lens, that cylindrical squeeze can produce oval bokeh, directional flares, and distinctive edge behavior; a wide aspect ratio alone is not anamorphic photography.

**Prompt:** Anamorphic 2x on [Subject], oval bokeh, horizontal flares, widescreen squeeze character.

### Spherical

**Definition:** A spherical lens in spherical lens cinematography uses rotationally symmetric elements with no anamorphic squeeze, so the image is captured already in its display proportions. Bokeh from a round iris is round, and edges stay rectilinear aside from ordinary wide-angle stretch.

**Prompt:** Spherical prime on [Subject], round bokeh, rectilinear edges, clean modern capture, no anamorphic streak.

### Fisheye

**Definition:** A fisheye in fisheye cinematography is a very wide curvilinear projection that bends straight lines, especially away from the center. The world wraps around the lens; architecture becomes a bowl rather than a rectilinear box.

**Prompt:** Fisheye on [Subject], circular or extreme barrel distortion, a world wrapping around a face, subjective unreality.

### Tilt-Shift

**Definition:** Tilt-shift cinematography tilts the lens relative to the sensor so the plane of focus rotates, and may also shift the lens to slide perspective. A narrow band or shaped region of sharpness, often from a high viewpoint, can make a real-scale scene resemble a small model.

**Prompt:** A real-scale city or citadel with [Subject] human-sized, tilt-shift with a thin focus plane, high viewpoint, miniature-model illusion.

### Macro

**Definition:** Macro cinematography is close-focusing optics that let a small object or surface occupy the image at high reproduction scale, often near 1:1. An unusually close view turns texture, insects, eyes, or mechanisms into the entire shot.

**Prompt:** Macro lens on a detail of [Subject], insects of texture, an eye, a watch, a drop of rain, a world in a centimeter.

### Probe Lens

**Definition:** A probe lens is a long, narrow macro optic that can pass through small spaces and create extreme close moving shots from inside the subject. The front element is tiny and far from the camera body, so the viewpoint can enter a glass, a wound of machinery, food, or a miniature set.

**Prompt:** Probe-lens view of [Subject], tiny wide lens on a stick, impossible proximity through glass, food, or machinery, miniature world.

### Split Diopter

**Definition:** A split diopter is a partial close-focus element that keeps different distance zones sharp in one frame. One region focuses very near while another remains focused farther away; it is not two focal lengths, and it is not ordinary deep focus.

**Prompt:** One continuous room: a near object on the left tack-sharp and [Subject] far on the right also tack-sharp, a narrow blur band between them.

### Vintage Cine Glass

**Definition:** Vintage cine glass is older motion-picture optics whose coatings, contrast, and aberrations differ from modern clinical primes. Lower contrast, gentle halation, and sometimes swirling or rolling edges are properties of the lens, not a grade slapped on sharp digital.

**Prompt:** Vintage cine glass on [Subject], lower contrast, gentle halation, swirling edges, Cooke or K35 character, not clinical sharpness.

### Telephoto Compression

**Definition:** Telephoto compression in lens compression cinematography is the perspective effect of a distant viewpoint: size differences between separated depth planes shrink, so a crowd or a city appears to press against a person. A long lens then frames that distant view tightly; a long lens from a close stand does not create the effect.

**Prompt:** Telephoto compression stacking [Subject] against a wall of background, distances collapsed, crowded air, no escape in depth.

### Magnification

**Definition:** Magnification in magnification cinematography is the ratio of the subject's image size on the sensor to its real size. High magnification makes a centimeter of the world fill the frame; it is a scale number, not a shot-size name and not automatically a macro lens.

**Prompt:** Extreme magnification of [Subject], a world inside a centimeter, scale lost on purpose.
