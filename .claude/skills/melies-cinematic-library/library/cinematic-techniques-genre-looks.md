### Film Noir

**Definition:** Film noir lighting is a low-key, high-contrast setup: a hard key, often cut by a venetian gobo or a streetlamp cookie, leaves one cheek in true black while wet pavement returns silver. The camera sits at eye height on a 35mm or 50mm spherical lens, tungsten practicals as motivated sources, and blocking that isolates a figure in a doorway, an alley, or a pool of light.

**Prompt:** Film-noir treatment of [Subject], hard venetian gobos, unfilled far cheek, wet-down street bounce, 50mm spherical, 1940s crime blocking.

### Neo-Noir

**Definition:** Neo-noir cinematography keeps noir's hard keys and isolated blocking but replaces silver night with color: sodium vapor, neon practicals, and tungsten that stain rain and skin. The camera still hunts faces in shadow, yet the palette is contemporary, often cyan signage against warm flesh, not black-and-white contrast alone.

**Prompt:** Neo-noir look on [Subject], color where classic noir was silver, neon practicals, rain, contemporary crime blocking, hard key, unfilled far cheek. Name the hue of the signage and whether the camera is locked or tracking.

### German Expressionism

**Definition:** German Expressionism cinematography treats the set as a mind: forced-perspective corridors, painted shadows, and jagged hard light replace natural architecture. The camera is often locked and slightly dutch, so the actor walks a geometry that already looks illegal.

**Prompt:** German expressionist treatment of [Subject], painted shadows, impossible architecture, jagged hard light, dutch horizon, theatrical blocking. Name which wall or stair is illegally angled.

### Giallo

**Definition:** Giallo cinematography is Italian thriller grammar: hyper-saturated primaries from gelled windows and glass, fashion interiors, and a camera that often zooms rather than dollies toward threat. Beauty lighting sits on the victim. The violence is styled, not silver.

**Prompt:** Giallo look on [Subject], hyper-saturated primaries, gelled window key, black leather implied in the foreground, 35mm zoom threat, Argento color crime. Name the red, green, or cobalt source.

### Spaghetti Western

**Definition:** Spaghetti western cinematography restages western iconography on Techniscope 2.35: dusty ochre landscapes, hard sun as the only key, and cuts from a 24mm plaza wide to a telephoto extreme close-up of eyes. Moral ambiguity sits in the hold on a face before anyone draws.

**Prompt:** Dusty ochre, hard noon sun, no fill, extreme close-up of eyes cut against a 24mm ridge. Name the dust and the holster height.

### Found Footage

**Definition:** The found footage look presents images as a recording made by someone inside the events, often later discovered or assembled, so the camera is a story object with a timestamp, a battery, and a reason to keep rolling. Consumer digital, Hi8, or MiniDV optics, autofocus hunt, and amateur framing are constraints, not decoration.

**Prompt:** Found-footage camera on [Subject], consumer digital or Hi8, timestamp, autofocus hunt, amateur framing, operator-height, documentary panic. Name who holds the camera and why it stays on.

### Cinéma Vérité

**Definition:** Cinéma vérité uses available light, zoom-to-find framing, and the filmmaker's presence as a catalyst, so the camera is admitted into the room rather than hidden. Unpretty 16mm or digital frames, mixed color temperature, and a zoom hunting the moment are the physical signature, not a recovered amateur tape.

**Prompt:** Cinéma vérité treatment of [Subject], available light, unpretty frames, zooming to find, mixed color temperature, life unstaged. Name the window or practical.

### French New Wave

**Definition:** French New Wave cinematography shoots fiction on locations with street light, handheld 16mm or 35mm, and an edit that will jump-cut rather than cover. The frame jumps into walking life: cafes, sidewalks, apartments with mixed practicals, and a camera that admits it is a camera.

**Prompt:** French New Wave energy on [Subject], location street light, handheld 35mm, jump cuts implied, jump-into-life framing, mixed cafe practicals. Name the sidewalk or apartment.

### Wuxia

**Definition:** Wuxia cinematography stages martial chivalry in landscape: misted bamboo or desert silk, long-shot geography, and wire-assisted paths that read as weightless rather than as street punching. Color is costume and fog, not a gelled murder window.

**Prompt:** Wuxia look on [Subject], misted bamboo or silk banners, wire-fu weightlessness implied, long-shot landscape, designed costume color, crane or wide hold. Name the weather and the path through air.

### Tech Noir

**Definition:** Tech noir (also called future noir or cyber-noir) fuses noir's hard keys, rain, and guilty blocking with science-fiction production design: screens, LEDs, and corporate towers as practical sources. Analog crime grammar sits inside a digital city.

**Prompt:** Tech-noir on [Subject], future crime, screens as practicals, rain, corporate night, hard unfilled key, analog noir in a digital city. Name the display that lights the face.

### Southern Gothic

**Definition:** Southern gothic cinematography uses the American South as a humid optical problem: late-afternoon gold gone sickly, Spanish-moss dappled light, porch tungsten, and atmospheric haze that keeps heat in the air. Decay is on the walls. The threat is family, history, and something under the porch, not a city streetlamp.

**Prompt:** Southern gothic air around [Subject], humid decay, Spanish moss dappled sun, late-afternoon gold gone sickly, porch tungsten, peeling paint. Name the heat and the house.

### Cosmic Horror

**Definition:** Cosmic horror cinematography builds dread from human insignificance: extreme long shots, slightly illegal geometry, under-light on faces, and a brightness that reveals too much. The threat is not a man. Scale, entity, and knowledge that will not fit a face are the optical problem.

**Prompt:** Cosmic horror framing of [Subject], scale wrong, geometry slightly illegal, under-lit face or a brightness that reveals too much, human irrelevance, extreme long shot or a corridor that does not vanish correctly. Do not prompt a gloved killer.

### Vaporwave

**Definition:** The vaporwave aesthetic is a pastel, low-contrast look: pink-cyan gradients, marble and palms, mall fluorescents, and VHS chroma bleed, as if a 1980s future never shipped. The camera is often centered and still. There is no crime geometry unless you add it.

**Prompt:** Vaporwave treatment of [Subject], pink-cyan gradient, marble and palms, VHS bleed, centered 35mm, mall fluorescent fill, no hard unfilled cheek. Name the bust or the grid.

### Documentary

**Definition:** Documentary cinematography mixes observational coverage with interview and archive rhetoric: unstyled wardrobe light, mixed color temperatures, a zoom finding focus, and respect without a glamour rim. Fictional films may borrow the same grammar as mockumentary. That is still documentary rhetoric, not a recovered camcorder tape.

**Prompt:** Documentary look on [Subject], mixed color, unstyled wardrobe light, zoom finding focus, interview or observational coverage, respect without glamour. Name whether this is a sit-down or a follow.

### Blockbuster Gloss

**Definition:** Blockbuster cinematography is high-contrast, billboard-readable coverage: clean skies or designed weather, a heroic rim on hair and shoulder, volumetric shafts, and a grade that often splits teal shadow from warm skin. The shot must still parse at poster size.

**Prompt:** Blockbuster gloss on [Subject], expensive contrast, clean skies, heroic rim, volumetric haze, IMAX-scale finish, adventure poster in motion. Name the rim and the horizon.

### Arthouse

**Definition:** Arthouse cinematography leaves space for thought: a static locked-off camera, odd headroom, natural or window light, and a frame willing to be empty. Duration is implied even in a still. The picture does not sell itself as a poster.

**Prompt:** Arthouse still of [Subject], patience, odd headroom, natural window light, the frame willing to be empty, static camera, duration implied. Name the unused air in the frame.

### Animation

**Definition:** Animation cinematography constructs light and motion instead of capturing them: graphic volume, designed keys, and movement as drawing, paint, or stepped CG, not as a photographed exposure. The world is built. Lens physics appear only if you choose them.

**Prompt:** Animated treatment of [Subject], graphic volume, designed light, motion as drawing rather than photography. Name the medium (halftone, charcoal, cel, graphic CG).

### BTS

**Definition:** Behind the scenes cinematography shows the making: C-stands, flags, the set wall, and crew in the same frame as the fiction, so the constructed world leaks. Documentary light (worklamps, mixed Kinos) sits beside the scene's key.

**Prompt:** Behind-the-scenes look at [Subject], crew, C-stands, and the set edge visible, the fiction leaking, worklight mixed with the scene key. Name a stand or a flag.

### Dreamcore

**Definition:** The dreamcore aesthetic is a soft, liminal interior look: empty rooms, pools, and suburban halls lit by childhood practicals gone slightly wrong, with low contrast and unresolved space. Nostalgia is present. Safety is not.

**Prompt:** Dreamcore image of [Subject], liminal rooms, soft fluorescent or blown window, childhood lighting gone wrong, low contrast, a memory that will not resolve. Name the empty hall or pool.

### Dystopian

**Definition:** Dystopian cinematography makes the civic system the antagonist: used architecture, haze, rank, and signage at a scale that already feels worn. The future is not shiny. It is bureaucratic, polluted, or monumental and cold, often in daylight as well as night.

**Prompt:** Dystopian view of [Subject], civic scale gone cold, haze, signage, and rank, used surfaces, a future that already feels worn. Name the checkpoint, queue, or tower.

### Magical Realism

**Definition:** Magical realism cinematography treats an extraordinary event as ordinary inside a recognizable world: the same window light, the same 35mm coverage, with one impossible fact staged as if it were weather. The camera does not gawk, add a rim, or warp the lens.

**Prompt:** Magical-realist image of [Subject], ordinary window or overcast light, 35mm eye level, one impossible fact treated as weather, no extra rim, no dutch. Name the ordinary room and the single impossibility.

### Maximalism

**Definition:** Maximalist cinematography uses density as the point: too much color, too much pattern, ornament in every zone, and lighting that keeps the clutter readable rather than hiding it. Restraint would be a lie, so the frame is packed on purpose.

**Prompt:** Maximalist frame of [Subject], too much color, too much pattern, multiple practicals, density as the point, 24mm to 40mm so the clutter stays readable. Name the wallpaper and the costume clash.

### Photography

**Definition:** Photographic cinematography treats the movie as a print: a single decisive frame, posed or found light, often centered composition or tableau blocking, with the camera pretending it could hang on a wall. Motion is allowed, but the still is the law.

**Prompt:** Still-photography grammar on [Subject], a single decisive frame, posed or window light, tripod, the movie pretending to be a print. Name the print logic (candle tableau, river still, posed portrait).

### Pixel Art

**Definition:** Pixel art builds the image from visible tiles or a low-resolution grid. The world is discrete squares, not continuous grain.

**Prompt:** Pixel-art render of [Subject], visible tiles, a low-resolution grid, discrete squares instead of continuous grain. Name the palette size and the resolution.

### Stylistic Suck

**Definition:** Stylistic suck is deliberate amateur cinematography: hunting zooms, cheap video, and the wrong white balance as a choice of the film's own camera, not necessarily of a character's recovered tape. Polish would be dishonest, so the image is ugly on purpose.

**Prompt:** Deliberate amateur look on [Subject], hunting zoom, cheap video, wrong white balance as a choice, blown practicals, bad centering. Do not add a timestamp unless there is also a diegetic camera.

### Video Game

**Definition:** Video game cinematography uses playable camera logic: a follow cam, an isometric view, or an FPS eyeline, with space readable as a level and a HUD implied. Collision, boom height, and pathing constrain the lens the way a game engine would.

**Prompt:** Video-game camera on [Subject], follow cam or isometric logic, HUD implied, space as a level, boom height locked, path readable. Name the camera type.

### Weirdcore

**Definition:** The weirdcore aesthetic renders the familiar incorrectly: early-web compression, low-res sacred images, off-center crops, and a joke that is also a threat. The file looks like it should not have opened. Light is on-camera flash or a dim analog room, not a designed key.

**Prompt:** Weirdcore image of [Subject], early-web dread, low-res sacred, wrong crop, JPEG artifacts, a joke that is also a threat. Name the caption or the chopped face.
