# Cinematic Techniques — Editing and Transitions (23 เทคนิก)

### Match Cut

**Definition:** A match cut joins two shots through a visual, spatial, sonic, or conceptual correspondence the viewer can read at the splice. The outgoing frame and the incoming frame share a shape, motion, object class, or idea, so the cut argues a relationship instead of only changing the view.

**Prompt:** Match cut from [Subject] to a rhyming shape or motion in another time, graphic continuity across a hard cut, bone to station.

### Graphic Match

**Definition:** A graphic match cut joins two images that share a dominant shape, line, color, placement, or motion pattern, even when location and time change. The outgoing contour and the incoming contour occupy the same part of the screen, so form carries you across the splice.

**Prompt:** Graphic match on the silhouette of [Subject], shape echoing shape, circle to circle, a visual rhyme. Specify the shared contour, its screen position, and that the objects stay distinct.

### Match on Action

**Definition:** Match on action cuts during one continuous action so the movement itself bridges two camera positions. The outgoing shot starts the gesture; the incoming shot finishes it, which is why the edit can change size or angle without feeling interrupted.

**Prompt:** Match on action through [Subject]'s gesture, the motion started in one shot and finished in the next. Name the wide and the close, the hand, the object, the direction of travel, and that the cut happens during the move.

### Jump Cut

**Definition:** A jump cut removes time or slightly shifts framing inside a similar setup, so the discontinuity is conspicuous. The outgoing frame and the incoming frame are close enough that the missing interval reads as a skip, not as a new shot.

**Prompt:** Jump cut on [Subject], time skipped inside a similar framing, interruption, anxiety or comedy of missing frames. Specify a locked or near-locked setup, what changes (posture, clock, crowd), and that the camera does not morph.

### Smash Cut

**Definition:** A smash cut makes an abrupt, high-contrast transition in image, sound, tone, scale, or time. The outgoing side has earned a state (quiet, prayer, resolution); the incoming side contradicts it without a polite blend.

**Prompt:** Smash cut from [Subject] to a violently different image, silence to noise, prayer to explosion, no polite dissolve. Name the outgoing state (quiet, dark, small, resolved) and the incoming opposite (loud, bright, huge, ruptured).

### Dissolve

**Definition:** A dissolve gradually overlaps the outgoing shot with the incoming shot so both images occupy the frame for a measurable duration. Opacity of A falls as opacity of B rises; at the midpoint you are looking at two ghosts.

**Prompt:** Dissolve from [Subject] into another time or place, overlapping ghosts, a longer time than a cut admits. Name both images, a shared hook if any (shape, motion, sound), and the mix duration.

### Fade In / Fade Out

**Definition:** A fade in or fade out takes the picture to or from a uniform field of black or color, rather than handing the frame straight to another visible scene. One side of the join is a world; the other side is absence, sleep, death, or a chapter break.

**Prompt:** Fade to or from black around [Subject], chapter punctuation, sleep, death, or the start of a world. Name the field color if it is not black.

### Wipe

**Definition:** A wipe replaces one image with another through a moving boundary that travels across the frame. One side of the join is still A; the other side is already B; the edge between them is the edit, and it is meant to be seen.

**Prompt:** Wipe replacing [Subject] with the next image, a line traveling across the frame, Star Wars grammar or a clock hand. Name direction, edge hardness, and both scenes.

### Iris

**Definition:** An iris transition reveals or conceals the picture with a moving circular or shaped mask. The mask's center is a pointer: one side of the join is the full world, the other is a hole closing on a face, a detail, or black.

**Prompt:** Iris in or out on [Subject], a circular mask closing on a face or detail, silent-era pointing, or a wink. Name the center (an eye, a ring, a cat), whether the mask follows, and whether it opens or closes.

### Cross-Cutting

**Definition:** Cross-cutting alternates two or more locations or action lines, often implying they are happening at the same time. Each side of the join is a complete strand; the engine is meanwhile, two clocks racing.

**Prompt:** Cross-cutting between [Subject] and a simultaneous other line, suspense by alternation, two clocks racing. Name both geographies, what the viewer is invited to assume, and whether that assumption is true.

### Split Screen

**Definition:** Split screen shows two or more image fields at once, so separate spaces share a single moment on the same frame. Both sides of the join are visible together; there is no waiting for the next cut.

**Prompt:** Split screen with [Subject] in one pane and another life in the other, simultaneous spaces, phone-call cinema or dual identity. Name the boundary, whether eyelines match, and what each pane contains.

### Montage

**Definition:** Film montage creates meaning or compression through the ordered juxtaposition of shots. Each side of a join is a complete image; the claim lives in the order, the collision, or the months squeezed into a minute.

**Prompt:** Montage sequence built around [Subject], time collapsed by images, training, travel, or a life in twenty shots. List the ordered images, what the collision claims, and whether time or idea is the engine.

### Axial Cut

**Definition:** An axial cut jumps closer or farther along approximately the same camera axis, changing shot size without changing angle. The outgoing frame and the incoming frame share a viewpoint; only distance snaps.

**Prompt:** Axial cut jumping toward or away from [Subject] along the lens axis, punch-in without a zoom, a snap of scale. Name two or three matched framings, same angle, same pose, hard cuts.

### Invisible Cut

**Definition:** An invisible cut hides an edit through motion, darkness, occlusion, blur, matching geometry, or digital stitching. One side of the join and the other are meant to feel like a single take; the splice is a seam the audience should not find.

**Prompt:** Invisible cut hidden in [Subject]'s whip, a dark wall, or a passing body, the oner illusion. Name the hiding action, the before plate, the after plate, and that the camera path should appear continuous.

### J-Cut / L-Cut (Visual Setup)

**Definition:** A J-cut or L-cut visual setup is a picture designed so sound can lead or lag across the splice. One side of the join is a face, a room, or a listener that can hold; the other side is a space already speaking, or still speaking after we have left it.

**Prompt:** A frame of [Subject] designed as the picture half of an L-cut or J-cut, reaction holding while another space is already speaking. For stills, describe a listening face or a doorway that can hold offscreen voice.

### Crash Cut

**Definition:** A crash cut slams two images together with no blend, so the join itself stings. One side of the splice and the other collide; unlike a smash that breaks an earned quiet, a crash cut often starts inside the event or hits like a drum from the first frame.

**Prompt:** Crash cut on [Subject], two images colliding with no polite blend, a hard join that should sting. Name A and B, the scale or sound collision, and that B may already be mid-event.

### Flash Cut

**Definition:** A flash cut interrupts the present image with a few frames of another picture, then returns or races on before the insert can be studied. One side of the join is the ongoing scene; the other is lightning: a thought, a future, a wound, lasting a blink.

**Prompt:** Flash cut through [Subject], a few frames of another image interrupting, lightning in the edit. Name the present scene, the insert (and its duration in frames), and the return.

### Match Motion

**Definition:** A match-motion cut continues direction and speed across the splice so the cut rides a vector even when location, time, or reality changes. One side of the join starts a movement; the other side keeps that movement, and the world around the body may be new.

**Prompt:** Match-motion cut on [Subject], direction and speed continuing across the join so the cut rides the movement. Name the outgoing path, the incoming path, matched pose and velocity, and what world changes.

### Match Split

**Definition:** A match split continues a graphic line or motion across a divide, so one contour belongs to two worlds at once. One side of the join is a pane or half-frame; the other is its partner; the shared edge, horizon, or gesture is the match.

**Prompt:** Match split of [Subject], a graphic divide that continues a line or motion into another space. Name the shared contour (horizon, table, eyeline), what each half contains, and that both are simultaneous.

### Quick Cuts

**Definition:** Quick cuts editing strings shots shorter than a breath, so duration itself becomes rhythm, pressure, or music. Each side of a join is a full view from a changing setup; the meaning is the rate, not a skip inside one frame.

**Prompt:** Quick cuts around [Subject], shots shorter than a breath, rhythm as violence or music. List a progression of sizes (wide to eyes, or hands to kit) and a duration curve.

### Set Transition

**Definition:** A set transition lets the location change in camera: walls walk, architecture rewrites, one space becomes another without a hard cut. Both sides of the join are geographies that share a continuous camera path; the set itself is the editor.

**Prompt:** Set transition around [Subject], the location transforming in camera, walls walking, one space becoming another. Name the starting architecture, the ending architecture, and that the camera does not hard-cut.

### Transitions

**Definition:** Film transitions are designed joins: the splice treated as a visible gesture rather than as continuity glue. One side of the join and the other are planned as a pair, and the audience is meant to notice how we left A and entered B.

**Prompt:** A designed transition around [Subject], the join as a gesture: wipe, morph, match, or light. Name both sides and the species of join.

### Fragments

**Definition:** Fragments, or fragmented editing, build an event from partial views, so the audience infers an action more complete than anything shown in one shot. Each side of a join is a shard: a blade, a mouth, a drain, a hand too late; the whole lives in the gaps.

**Prompt:** Fragments of [Subject], shattered coverage, pieces of a moment, the scene as shards. List the shards in causal order and name the missing master.
