### Lens Flare

**Definition:** Lens flare in lens flare cinematography is stray light scattered by glass, coatings, and barrel walls, producing ghosts, streaks, or veiling glare instead of a clean image. The artifacts live in the taking optic, so they track the source angle and dim when a body occludes the lamp.

**Prompt:** Lens flare across [Subject], the sun or a hard practical striking the front element, veiling contrast, ghost orbs tracking the source, source-linked not floating.

### Anamorphic Flare

**Definition:** An anamorphic flare is a horizontal streak produced when a bright point hits the cylindrical elements of an anamorphic lens. It is a subset of lens flare, not a general haze, and it usually arrives with oval bokeh from the same squeeze.

**Prompt:** Horizontal anamorphic flares streaking through [Subject], cyan or magenta bars from headlights or neon, oval bokeh, 2x squeeze, not round spherical ghosts.

### Halation

**Definition:** Halation in film is a reddish or warm glow around bright highlights caused by light passing through the emulsion, reflecting off the base, and scattering back into the image. It is a stock behavior, not a diffusion filter and not flare inside the lens.

**Prompt:** Halation glow around highlights on [Subject], red-edge bloom of film stock, lamps breathing, eyes and fabric texture preserved, not a global fog.

### Film Grain

**Definition:** Film grain in film grain cinematography is the random texture of silver halide crystals or color dye clouds in the emulsion, or a digital simulation of that structure. It is a developed surface, not a PNG overlay sitting on top of a clean render.

**Prompt:** Visible film grain over [Subject], 35mm or 16mm dye-cloud texture, temporally unstable, denser in shadows, not a cheap overlay.

### Bokeh

**Definition:** Bokeh in bokeh cinematography is the aesthetic character of out-of-focus rendering: the disc shape, edge, and smoothness of blurred points, not the mere fact of a thin depth of field. The glass, not a blur slider, decides whether those discs are round, oval, outlined, or swirling.

**Prompt:** Creamy bokeh field behind [Subject], circular or oval highlight discs from practicals, subject sharp, background as light not as a masked blur.

### Double Exposure

**Definition:** Double exposure in double exposure film combines two or more image records on one frame, in camera by winding back, optically in a printer, or digitally as an additive composite. Both pictures occupy the same emulsion or file unless a matte fully holds one off.

**Prompt:** Double exposure of [Subject] overlaid with a second named image, faces in cities, a ghost in rain, optical composite in camera, both exposures visible, not a morph and not a split-screen join.

### Rack Focus

**Definition:** A rack focus moves the sharp plane from one subject or distance to another during a continuous shot, while the composition stays largely the same. Attention changes because the helical turns, not because the camera cuts or dollies.

**Prompt:** Rack focus between planes around [Subject], a two-second pull from foreground to the figure or the reverse.

### Shallow Focus

**Definition:** Shallow focus keeps a narrow depth range sharp while foreground or background falls off. It is a depth-of-field choice made with distance, focal length, and aperture together, not a button labeled blur.

**Prompt:** Very shallow focus on [Subject], T1.4, near eye sharp, background dissolved into optical discs, intimacy by exclusion, not a cutout mask.

### Deep Focus

**Definition:** Deep focus in deep focus cinematography keeps multiple near and far planes legibly sharp in one image. It is a continuous depth of field, not merely action arranged in depth while the glass stays selective.

**Prompt:** Deep focus on [Subject] and the far plane, near action and distant doorway both sharp, no rack focus, no split-diopter seam, the eye may wander.

### Forced Perspective

**Definition:** Forced perspective uses camera position, subject distance, and set geometry to alter apparent size or distance, and the lie exists only from the taking viewpoint. Bodies, furniture, and eyelines must agree or the scale collapses.

**Prompt:** Forced perspective with [Subject], scale altered by camera placement and scaled furniture, giant or miniature in camera, locked viewpoint, matching eyelines, not a digital resize.

### Light Leak

**Definition:** A light leak in light leak film is unfiltered light that reaches the emulsion or sensor without passing through the taking lens, usually through a gap in the body, magazine, or gate. It fogs from the edge or along a scratch in the film path, often orange or red on color negative because it hits the base side.

**Prompt:** Analog light leak washing [Subject], orange or red fog eating the frame edge, unfiltered light on the emulsion, not a source-linked lens flare.

### Vignette

**Definition:** A vignette in vignette cinematography darkens, desaturates, or lowers contrast toward the frame edges because of optical falloff, mechanical vignetting in the barrel, a filter, or a later grade. Optical vignette follows the lens, while a post oval is a drawing.

**Prompt:** Natural optical vignette around [Subject], corners a stop down, attention pooled in the center, vintage falloff not a post oval.

### Chromatic Aberration

**Definition:** Chromatic aberration in chromatic aberration cinematography is color fringing from different wavelengths focusing at different distances or magnifications. Lateral CA puts red-cyan (or similar) fringes on high-contrast edges, worse toward the frame edge, while longitudinal CA puts different colors in front of and behind the focus plane.

**Prompt:** Chromatic aberration on [Subject], red-cyan fringing at high-contrast edges, stronger toward the corners, vintage or simple glass, not a full RGB channel offset.

### Infrared

**Definition:** Infrared cinematography photographs near-infrared reflectance (roughly 700 to 900 nm), not heat, so foliage often goes pale, skies go dark, and skin can look waxy. It is a different slice of reflected light, still a photograph of the location.

**Prompt:** Infrared look on [Subject], foliage going pale, skies black, skin waxy, near-infrared photograph, not night-vision green, not a thermal palette.

### Night Vision

**Definition:** Night vision in night vision cinematography is image intensification: residual photons, and often a near-IR illuminator, amplified through a photocathode and microchannel plate onto a phosphor. The picture is typically green, grainy, blooming around lamps, with the framing of goggles, a weapon sight, or a camera in the dark.

**Prompt:** Night-vision green phosphor of [Subject], grainy image intensification, blooming lamps, goggle or sight framing, surveillance or military eye, not a thermal palette.

### Thermal

**Definition:** Thermal imaging cinematography maps emitted long-wave infrared (heat), not reflected light. A microbolometer or similar sensor assigns a palette (white-hot, black-hot, ironbow) to temperature, so faces, engines, and blood read warm while cold metal and night air do not.

**Prompt:** Thermal palette of [Subject], heat as color, faces as warm masks, ironbow or white-hot, search-and-rescue or predator vision, not night-vision green, not pale infrared foliage.

### Slit-Scan

**Definition:** Slit-scan exposes moving artwork or a moving camera through a narrow slit over time, stretching space into streaks and corridors with time laid along one axis. The result is structured geometry, not a random particle tunnel.

**Prompt:** Slit-scan streaking around [Subject], time smeared along one axis, structured corridor of light with a stable vanishing point, Star Gate language, no random neon particles.

### Kaleidoscope

**Definition:** Kaleidoscope cinematography fragments the image with mirrors or radial repetition so one subject becomes a symmetric pattern. The camera looks into angled glass, or the staging itself is a kaleidoscope: bodies arranged around an axis, often from above.

**Prompt:** Kaleidoscopic fragmentation of [Subject], mirrored shards, radial symmetry, Busby Berkeley from inside a jewel, not a slit-scan tunnel.

### Light Flash

**Definition:** A light flash transition is a brief blowout of the frame to white or near-white, then a return, as if a bulb, explosion, or memory dump hit the emulsion. It is a temporal event, often a cut hidden in the peak, not a standing lens flare.

**Prompt:** Blinding light flash over [Subject], a bulb, explosion, or memory cut, the frame going to white then returning.

### Projections

**Definition:** Projections in projection mapping film put a pictured image onto a surface that the taking camera then photographs: front projection onto a retroreflective screen, rear projection behind a window or windshield, or video mapped onto architecture. The projector is a light source, and the set is also a screen.

**Prompt:** Projected imagery crawling over [Subject] and architecture, a plate on a screen or faces on walls, foreground lit to match the projection, in-camera process not a double-exposure ghost.

### Morph

**Definition:** A morphing effect transforms one image, face, shape, or object into another by interpolating corresponding geometry over time. It is a continuous becoming, not a crossfade of opacities and not two exposures stacked.

**Prompt:** Morph between [Subject] and another named state, face or body becoming, in-between geometry visible.

### Altered State

**Definition:** Altered state cinematography makes the mind the location: smear, afterimage, wrong color, unstable time, optics and cutting that no sober observer would report. It is a bundle of techniques motivated by consciousness, not a single filter name.

**Prompt:** Altered-state image of [Subject], smear, afterimage, wrong color, time that will not sit still.

### Anthropo

**Definition:** Anthropomorphic cinematography stages an object or animal as a performer: human scale in the frame, a gesture, an eyeline, reaction shots, as if the inanimate had a will. The camera does not need a face so much as an actor's grammar.

**Prompt:** Anthropomorphic frame of [Subject], an object or animal given human scale, gesture, or eyeline, singles and looks as if it had a will, not a camera inside the object.

### Cinemagraph

**Definition:** A cinemagraph is a still photograph in which one isolated element continues to move on a loop (steam, rain on glass, hair, a blink) while the rest of the frame is frozen. It is not a freeze-frame of a whole shot, and it is not a full clip that happens to loop.

**Prompt:** Cinemagraph of [Subject], almost a still, one isolated motion looping: steam, hair, a blink, rain in a window.

### Collage

**Definition:** Collage cinematography builds a frame from torn, cut, or mixed pieces: different stocks, paper, print, and live action sharing one plane with visible joins. Edges are graphic, and the stack is a construction, not an optical blend.

**Prompt:** Collage frame of [Subject], torn layers, mixed stocks, cut paper and live action sharing one plane, visible edges, not a smooth optical overlay.

### Datamosh

**Definition:** Datamosh is intentional codec failure: inter-frame prediction continues after keyframes are removed or broken, so motion vectors smear the previous picture into the next GOP and faces bleed into the following shot. The image is file wreckage, not a lens error.

**Prompt:** Datamosh of [Subject], inter-frame collapse, faces smearing into the next GOP, digital ruin as style.

### Diorama

**Definition:** Diorama cinematography photographs a physical scale model so that it stands in for a much larger object or environment, with real light hitting real miniature surfaces. Scale is sold by texture, camera speed, depth of field, and movement, not by a toy-like tilt of focus.

**Prompt:** Diorama view of [Subject], physical miniature, tactile surfaces, deep enough focus for full-size scale, slow camera, not tilt-shift toy blur.

### Distortions

**Definition:** Lens distortion cinematography warps space so straight lines bow, stretch, or melt: barrel, pincushion, mustache, extreme wide stretch, or a physical distorting optic such as a diopter, a bottle, or rain on glass. The lens is lying about geometry, not adding flare.

**Prompt:** Optical distortion around [Subject], stretched edges, barrel or melted geometry from ultra-wide or fisheye glass close to the face, the lens lying about space, not a flare pack.

### Duplication

**Definition:** A duplicate subject effect puts the same performer in a frame more than once as solid bodies: twins, a self across a table, copies in one room. It is usually a split matte, motion control, or a locked-camera composite, not a translucent double exposure.

**Prompt:** Duplication of [Subject], the same figure twice in one frame, twins of identity, solid bodies with matched light and contact shadows, not a double-exposure ghost, not a visible split-screen line.

### Echo Print

**Definition:** An echo print is an echo print effect that leaves a ghosted afterimage offset in time or register, so the last pose is still printed on this frame. The ghost is a stain of a previous exposure, not a second complete picture sharing the same plane.

**Prompt:** Echo print of [Subject], a ghosted afterimage offset in time or register, the last frame still printed on this one.

### Feedback

**Definition:** Video feedback is made by pointing a camera at its own live output so the picture nests into a recursive corridor of the same face. Each generation is a rephotograph of the monitor, with bloom, lag, and a tunnel of shrinking frames.

**Prompt:** Video feedback around [Subject], camera looking into its own output, infinite corridor of the same face.

### Floating UI

**Definition:** Floating UI, or HUD overlay cinematography, places readable interface, callouts, and data planes in the air of the scene so information lives in space, not only on a physical screen. The cockpit is the room.

**Prompt:** Floating UI over [Subject], HUD, callouts, and interface planes in 3D space, the screen as a cockpit. Name what the panels say in plain language, keep type sharp, and say the planes parallax if the camera moves.

### Focal Shift

**Definition:** A focal shift moves the plane of sharpness from one subject or distance to another during the shot, so attention travels as a pull. The camera body can stay put, and only the focus mechanism walks the depth.

**Prompt:** Focal shift on [Subject], the plane of sharpness traveling from one fact to another, attention as a pull.

### Generative

**Definition:** Generative visuals show process: forms grown by rule, particles, and iteration, so cinema is made by a system rather than only by a finished photograph. Making is visible.

**Prompt:** Generative image of [Subject], forms grown by rule, particles, and iteration, cinema made by a system. Name the rule and the light on the bits.

### Levitation

**Definition:** A levitation shot holds a body or object in a quiet impossible hover, refusing ordinary gravity while support is hidden or removed. Hair and cloth still hang, and the gap under the feet is the proof.

**Prompt:** Levitation of [Subject], body or object refusing gravity, a quiet impossible hover.

### Masking

**Definition:** Masking cinematography uses a matte, a viewfinder object, or a graphic window so looking is visible as a shape cutting the picture. We see through binoculars, a keyhole, or a hard-edged hole.

**Prompt:** Masked frame around [Subject], a shape cutting the picture, binoculars, a keyhole, a graphic window. Name the shape, keep the interior photographed, and keep the surround opaque.

### Mixed Media

**Definition:** Mixed media film combines live action with type, illustration, and stock in one plane so no single capture method owns the truth of the frame. Media collide rather than dissolve into one photograph.

**Prompt:** Mixed media around [Subject], live action, type, illustration, and stock colliding in one plane. Name each medium.

### Morphing

**Definition:** Morphing cinematography transforms one shape or image continuously into another by interpolating geometry, rather than merely fading between pictures. Identity is a dissolve you can watch as contour.

**Prompt:** Morph of [Subject] into another state, continuous transformation, identity as a dissolve you can watch.

### Object Portal

**Definition:** An object portal effect treats a prop as a wormhole: a bag, a screen, or a doorway that should not lead there, opening onto another space. The thing is the cut.

**Prompt:** Object portal through [Subject], a thing opening onto another space, a bag, a screen, a doorway that should not lead there. Name the prop, the rim, and the far geography inside it.

### Photogrammetry

**Definition:** The photogrammetry look reconstructs 3D geometry and texture from overlapping photographs, so a real place is turned into a scanned mesh. Slightly wrong normals, stretched texture, and holes are the evidence of capture.

**Prompt:** Photogrammetry look of [Subject], scanned space, slightly wrong mesh, a real place turned into a model. Name overlapping-photo texture, a hole or stretch, and an orbit or inspection camera.

### Ratio Switch

**Definition:** An aspect ratio switch changes the image's visible boundaries so the container itself widens or closes as the story changes register. The picture is not stretched: previously unseen area is revealed, or the crop tightens.

**Prompt:** Aspect-ratio switch on [Subject], the frame itself widening or closing as the story changes register.

### Scale Shift

**Definition:** Scale shift cinematography changes a body's size against the same room so the figure is suddenly giant or tiny: Alice physics as an optical event. Furniture, shadows, and contact have to match the new ratio.

**Prompt:** Scale shift of [Subject], the body suddenly giant or tiny against the same room, Alice physics. Name the room, the new size, and matching shadows.

### Shadow Box

**Definition:** Shadow-box cinematography tells the story in layered cut-outs, depth as stacked silhouettes, a diorama of darkness. Figures are unfilled, and distance is paper stacked in front of a hard backlight.

**Prompt:** Shadow-box of [Subject], layered cut-outs, depth as stacked silhouettes, a diorama of darkness. Name several planes, a hard backlight, and no fill on the figures.

### Transformation

**Definition:** Transformation cinematography makes a body, room, or object become something else in continuous view. The camera keeps watching while the state changes, with a stable anchor so the becoming can be read.

**Prompt:** Transformation of [Subject], a body, room, or object becoming something else in continuous view.

### Typography

**Definition:** Kinetic typography puts words in the world of the shot as objects: titles that occupy space, letters with scale, parallax, and a surface. The sentence is in the picture, not parked as a caption.

**Prompt:** Typography in the world of [Subject], words as objects, titles that occupy space, kinetic type. Name the words, the surface they sit on, and the light.

### Wigglegram

**Definition:** A wigglegram is a short stereo shiver: two or three frames rocking so depth reads as a fidget. The viewpoints are a few centimeters apart, so depth is a twitch, not a dolly.

**Prompt:** Wigglegram of [Subject], two or three frames rocking, a cheap stereo shiver, depth as a fidget.

### X-Ray

**Definition:** X-ray cinematography images through a surface as if denser structure were visible: bones, wiring, a body as diagram. Transmission, not reflectance, is the physics.

**Prompt:** X-ray view of [Subject], interior structure, bones or wiring, a body as diagram. Demand transmission, a readable skeleton or circuit, and no thermal palette.

### Zoetrope

**Definition:** Zoetrope cinematography shows the frames inside a movement: stroboscopic slices of a spinning action, motion as a drum of poses. You see the sampling, not a smooth blur.

**Prompt:** Zoetrope of [Subject], stroboscopic slices of a spinning action, motion as a drum of frames.

### Focus Change

**Definition:** Focus change cinematography jumps or slides the plane of sharpness to a new fact, a rack without apology. The mid-blur is short, and the new plane owns the shot as soon as it lands.

**Prompt:** Focus change on [Subject], the plane of sharpness jumping or sliding to a new fact, a rack without apology.

### Floating Fall

**Definition:** A floating fall is a body hanging in a slow drop: delayed gravity on hair and coat, still going down. The path remains toward the ground, even when the clock on the body is late.

**Prompt:** Floating fall of [Subject], a body hanging in slow drop, weightless disaster, hair and coat in delayed gravity.

### Smash and Grab

**Definition:** Smash-and-grab video is impact as coverage: glass, snatch, handheld chaos, a theft of a moment. The camera is inside the crime, too close and too late.

**Prompt:** Smash-and-grab energy on [Subject], glass, impact, snatch, handheld chaos, a theft of a moment.

### Cyclope

**Definition:** A cyclope camera is one giant iris looking: fisheye as a monster eye, a single hole, too close. The lens is the creature.

**Prompt:** Cyclope view of [Subject], a single giant iris, fisheye as a monster eye, one hole looking. Demand barrel distortion, a catchlight, and a circular hole.

### Particles

**Definition:** Particle effects cinematography fills the air with countable bits: dust, sparks, pollen, a weather of motes, each one catching light. Atmosphere you can inventory, not a blanket of fog.

**Prompt:** Particles around [Subject], dust, sparks, pollen, a weather of bits, light catching each one. Name the species, a backlight, and that the bits are discrete.

### Argus

**Definition:** An Argus image, argus many eyes, fills the frame with looking: extra pupils on a body, a swarm of viewpoints, a creature or a city of eyes. The surplus is seeing, counted as organs or cameras, not as flare.

**Prompt:** Argus image of [Subject], many eyes, many viewpoints, a creature or a city of looking. Count the eyes.

### Sticker Peel

**Definition:** A sticker peel effect lifts the image from a backing: adhesive shine, a die-cut layer coming off the world. Thickness, curl, and glue are the physics, not a still collage of torn paper.

**Prompt:** Sticker peel on [Subject], the image lifting from a backing, adhesive shine, a graphic coming off the world.

### Selfie Twin

**Definition:** A selfie twin puts two of the same face in a phone-arm frame, identity as a double take at arm's length. The camera is a compact wide in a hand, not a hidden studio body.

**Prompt:** Selfie twin of [Subject], two of the same face in a phone-arm frame, identity as a double take. Demand the same person twice, compact-camera distortion, and a phone-arm wide.

### Bubbles

**Definition:** Bubbles cinematography fills the air with little lenses: soap films that refract the subject, rainbow edges, wet highlights. Each bubble is an optic, not a disk of bokeh.

**Prompt:** Bubbles around [Subject], refraction worlds that still read as joy or threat. Demand soap-film surfaces, rainbow edges, and a repeated face in the spheres.
