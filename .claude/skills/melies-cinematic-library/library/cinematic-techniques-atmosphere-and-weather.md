### Rain

**Definition:** Rain in film is falling water that makes air visible as streaks and turns the ground into a mirror. Drops are matter in the optical path; without backlight they vanish, and without a wet-down the street stays dry under a fake storm.

**Prompt:** Heavy rain around [Subject], backlit drops, wet-down pavement, umbrellas, silver streaks, weather as percussion. Name the backlight (a streetlamp, a sign, a HMI behind the rain), the wet ground, and drop size.

### Fog

**Definition:** Fog is a dense local suspension of water droplets that collapses depth, turns lamps into globes, and delays the reveal of figures. The air is matter close to camera; geography hides until someone walks into the last few meters.

**Prompt:** Dense fog around [Subject], depth collapsing, lamps becoming globes, figures appearing late. Name density (visibility in meters), the practicals blooming, and what is hidden.

### Mist

**Definition:** Mist is a thinner water suspension than fog: pale layered air that still lets you read the next plane. Droplets are matter, but the veil is lyric, a morning or mountain breath rather than a wall.

**Prompt:** Light mist around [Subject], softer than fog, morning or mountain breath, layers of pale air. Name near-subject readability, paler far planes, and a backlight or low sun.

### Atmospheric Haze

**Definition:** Atmospheric haze is scattering over distance: far planes go paler and often cooler, so kilometers of air become the depth cue. The particles are too small to read as drops; the air is matter you measure in ridges, not in globes around a near lamp.

**Prompt:** Atmospheric haze stacking distance around [Subject], aerial perspective, far planes paler, depth as weather. Keep the near subject contrasty; name two or three receding planes.

### Smoke

**Definition:** Smoke is combustion particles with a source, a direction, and enough density to turn a room's air into a volume the light can strike. Unlike weather fog, it has a chimney, a cigarette, a tire, or a battlefield; backlight carves it into shafts and swirls.

**Prompt:** Smoke around [Subject], cigarette, chimney, or battle, shafts of light catching it, volume in the room. Name the source, the backlight or side key, and the direction of the ribbon.

### Dust Motes

**Definition:** Dust motes are discrete particles hanging in a shaft of light, slow enough to read as time in still air. They are matter you can almost count; they live in the beam, not as a veil across the whole room.

**Prompt:** Dust motes hanging in a shaft of light around [Subject], slow particles, a room that has been closed, time made visible. Name the window or slat, the dark surround, and that particles are discrete, not a fog fill.

### Steam

**Definition:** Steam is water vapor released where heat meets colder air: trains, manholes, showers, a white veil that rises and thins. It is wet matter, not combustion; it billows, condenses on lens and wool, and has a thermal source.

**Prompt:** Steam billowing around [Subject], trains, manholes, showers, a white veil, industrial or intimate heat. Name the hot source, the cold air, backlight, and rise.

### Snow

**Definition:** Snow is ice falling and settling: large flakes in backlight, a muffled world, tracks that record who passed. The air holds crystals you can size; the ground accumulates; contrast goes soft because the world has been covered.

**Prompt:** Falling snow around [Subject], large flakes in backlight, muffled world, tracks, winter silence you can see. Name flake size, a lamp or sun behind them, accumulation on shoulders and ground, and tracks if anyone has walked.

### Wet-Down

**Definition:** A wet-down sprays pavement, asphalt, and cars so night lights double themselves in reflection, often with no rain in the air. The street is the effect; water is a surface treatment, not weather.

**Prompt:** Wet-down streets around [Subject], pavement sprayed for night, double the lights by reflection. Name the practicals, the sheeting water on asphalt, and empty air unless rain is also requested.

### Sparks and Embers

**Definition:** Sparks and embers are incandescent particles thrown by welding, fire, grinding, or a cigarette: orange weather with short trajectories. They are burning matter; backlight is optional because they are their own small keys.

**Prompt:** Sparks or embers around [Subject], welding, fire, cigarettes, orange particles as weather. Name the source, color (white-orange to dull red), trajectory (fountain versus lofted drift), and a dark surround.

### Dust and Sand

**Definition:** Dust and sand as weather is mineral grit driven by wind until the sun is a dull coin and figures become silhouettes. The land attacks: particles occupy near air, sting highlights, and erase the horizon that Atmospheric Haze would only pale.

**Prompt:** Dust or sandstorm wrapping [Subject], sun as a dull coin, silhouettes, grit in the highlights. Name wind direction, reduced visibility, and dirty speculars on skin and metal.

### Underwater

**Definition:** Underwater cinematography leaves air: the camera is in water, so caustics, buoyancy, and a missing horizon rewrite every edge. The medium is the matter; particles are silt and bubbles, and bodies move as if the world had gone slow without a slow-motion shutter.

**Prompt:** Underwater of [Subject], caustics, muffled space, hair and cloth in slow water, a world without horizon. Name depth, the surface as ceiling or absence, floating wardrobe, silt or bubbles in backlight.

### Ocean

**Definition:** Ocean cinematography treats the sea as the other actor: swell, a horizon, salt light, a person against water that does not care. The atmosphere is spray, haze over distance, and a moving floor; the camera is in air, on a hull, or on rocks, not yet in the lung of the sea.

**Prompt:** Ocean world of [Subject], swell, horizon, salt light, a person against water that does not care. Name sea state, scale (small figure or small boat), backlight on spray, and a hard horizon.
